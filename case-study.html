Functional BA case study · Figma + FigJam

Getting field staff paid in 3 days instead of 14

Halden Logistics ran expense claims on an Excel template, email approvals and manual ERP entry. I mapped the process, traced the delay to five handoffs, and designed a digital claim flow with a policy engine and ERP integration.

Step 1 · Discovery

Who cares, and how much

I ran 11 stakeholder interviews and shadowed two AP clerks through a full week's claims. The power/interest grid set the engagement plan: the CFO and AP Lead got weekly reviews, line managers got demos, and Audit got sign-off on the policy rules.

Step 2 · Current state

The delay lives in the handoffs

The as-is BPMN swimlane makes the problem visible: two rework loops send claims back to the employee, and four of the five pain points sit on a lane boundary. Each one is numbered so it can be traced through the rest of the project.

Step 3 · Future state

Route by risk, not by habit

The to-be process captures receipts with OCR, checks every line against policy at the point of entry, and routes the claim by risk band. Low-risk claims skip the manager. Finance only sees exceptions. Approved claims post to the ERP by API and pay daily.

Step 4 · Persona journey

Priya's worst moment is a Sunday spreadsheet

The journey map placed the emotional low at claim preparation, not at payment. That moved OCR auto-fill to the top of the backlog, ahead of the payment-batch change Finance had asked for first.

Step 5 · Solution design

Wireframes that carry the requirements

I built mid-fidelity screens from a small component library so the design team could start from real structure. Every screen is tagged with the requirement IDs it satisfies, and the review screen shows business rule BR-07 failing in context.

Step 6 · Specification

Every requirement traces both ways

32 backlog items, prioritised with MoSCoW. Stories carry Gherkin acceptance criteria that QA turned straight into test cases. The traceability matrix links each item back to a pain point and forward to a screen and a UAT case.

Step 7 · Outcomes

Pilot results and the business case

Two regions and 46 claimants ran the new flow for eight weeks. Cycle time reached the 3-day target by week 8. The annual case nets ₹9.8 lakh after licence and integration costs, with a nine-month payback.
